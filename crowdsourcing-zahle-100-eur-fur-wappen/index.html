Crowdsourcing! Pay 100,- € for a Coat of Arms

April 1, 2010
tech

This website is called gartnerich.com because it belongs to the Gartner family. And we Gartners are nobility – well, sort of… In itself, that’s not particularly spectacular, because as long as you don’t have a “von” in your name (or even better, a “Graf”), it doesn’t mean much. However, there is one thing you get: A coat of arms! Our coat of arms looks like this:

Our patent of nobility dates back to 15.11.1699, and it describes the coat of arms roughly as follows:

…Noble coat of arms to be used and carried henceforth eternally, graciously granted and permitted as follows: “a quartered shield, the rear lower and front upper sections divided into three equal bars, the rear and front are white, but the middle is red; from the front lower to the rear upper corner cut diagonally with a stripe, red in white and white in red, on each elongated white bar one on the red two hexagonal golden stars, but in the diagonal stripe in the red a white and in the white a red rose can be seen. Front lower and rear upper sections, divided in the middle transversely, so that the lower part is yellow or gold-colored, the upper part blue or azure, and in the lower part on a green three-humped hill a brown horse standing upright for a jump, saddled with a flying bridle, in the rear upper part a standing griffin appears, also on a humped hill, blue or azure from bottom to middle, but yellow or gold-colored in the upper part, with open wings, a red protruding tongue, and holding a curved saber by the hilt with the right front claw, but holding the tip with the left claw. In the middle of the quartering a white or silver-colored crowned heart shield, in which a miner with a brown beard and hair, dressed in a tight red coat and on the head with a Hungarian hanging cap, with the brim, belt, buttons, and coat lapel white or silver-colored, setting the left hand on the hip, but holding in the right a miner’s step, from which two green palm branches, surrounded by a laurel wreath, grow out. On the shield a free, noble tournament helmet with surrounding jewels, etc. etc.”

Now I would like to use the coat of arms – or something similar to it. However, I have only found a small photo in our family history. Moreover, the coat of arms is quite ornate; I would prefer something simpler, more streamlined. Hence the following call:

Whoever designs a beautiful coat of arms for us will be rewarded with 100,- EUR!

Here are the rules:

  • The coat of arms must appeal to me – so much so that I use it. In other words: If I use it, I pay.
  • Only the coat of arms that I like best will be rewarded.
  • I receive all usage rights to the coat of arms. This means, among other things, that the artist has transferred all rights because they own them.
  • Explicitly again: It must be a truly self-developed work, nothing copied.
  • I will definitely wait until the end of April, not necessarily longer.

The technical requirements:

  • One or more PSD files must be delivered.
  • It should be usable both large and small, or include variants for large and small usage. An example of large usage would be a print on the back of a T-shirt. A small usage would be on a golf ball.

And here are a few bullet points with hints about what I like (or don’t):

  • I imagine the modernization to be less ornate in detail.
  • It can be quirky, but not messy.
  • You don’t have to stick strictly to the model; omissions are allowed. However, it should still be reminiscent of the Gartner coat of arms.
  • Example uses for the coat of arms would be
  • On the flag of our bike trailer (size approx. DIN A4)
  • On T-shirts or polos, large on the back or small on the sleeve.
  • On this website.
  • On golf balls or skis.
  • I’m sure I’ll think of more…

So now it’s your turn. I’m curious to see what comes in – and if anything comes in at all…

Good night, – Till.
