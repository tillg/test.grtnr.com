India Day 2

April 11, 2012

Today was our second day in India – once again very exciting!

Here is the photo series:

After having a good sleep and feeling adjusted to the local rhythm (meaning the jet lag was OK), we took the metro to Old Delhi. As the name suggests, this is the old part of Delhi…

Old Delhi feels much more like how many people described India to me before my departure: Another era…

Two things that strongly shape the impressions, which are not visible in the photos, are:

  1. The noise: All rickshaws, cars, trucks, even bicycles honk and ring constantly and extremely loudly. Since the streets in Old Delhi have no separation between roadway and sidewalk, you’re constantly jumping aside to avoid being run over or bumped into.

  2. The smells: Everything has a scent – mostly not pleasant…

As you can see, the IT industry in India is omnipresent: It also strongly influences the street scene here ;-)

Many people wash themselves on the street here – maybe because it’s Sunday?

…and people also sleep in cars – or something like that…

You also see animals everywhere on the street

And right in the middle – a temple… This time a mosque.

In the mosque, it feels like whole families live here:

And this is how the mosque looks from the entrance area, across the large square:

Afterwards, we went back into the hustle and bustle of the alleys of Old Delhi

…where everything is sold…

…and you can find everything – including services…

…but also spectacular spice shops (and this isn’t even the spice market!)

Or fabrics (somewhere the saris have to come from)

Looking up is always astonishing…

Then we went to the Red Fort. That is…a temple…

Did I mention that the temple complexes here are often huge?!

…and it feels like construction is going on at every corner all the time?

After so many temples, we got hungry – but didn’t want to join the gentlemen eating directly on the street, so we went to a nice tourist restaurant…

Then we briefly visited an area influenced by the English colonial style: Connaught Place.

Afterwards, we took the metro back to the hotel, showered, and met Indian colleagues for dinner. Now it’s just before midnight here, and I’m going to sleep.

Good night,

– Till.
